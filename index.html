NASA HUMAN RESEARCH PROGRAM (HRP) FRAMEWORK

The 5 Hazards of Human Spaceflight (R-I-D-G-E)

NASA's official clinical risk categorization for long-duration deep-space missions to the Moon and Mars.

NASA HRP EVIDENCE BASE
R HAZARD 01 // DOSIMETRY

Space Radiation

Galactic Cosmic Rays (GCR) and Solar Particle Events (SPE). Increases lifetime carcinogenesis, cataracts, and CNS degradation. Shielding and storm shelter protocols tracked continuously.

ACTIVE DOSIMETRY: 0.42 mSv/d
I HAZARD 02 // BEHAVIORAL

Isolation & Confinement

Circadian rhythm disruption across 16 sunrises/day, psychological friction, sleep deprivation. Monitored via PVT alertness reaction latency and melatonergic lighting spectrum.

PVT ALERTNESS: 218 ms (OPTIMAL)
D HAZARD 03 // AUTONOMY

Distance from Earth

Up to 22.4-minute one-way comms delay to Mars. Real-time ground telemedicine is impossible. Demands autonomous on-board Clinical Decision Support Systems (ExMC CDSS) like AURA.

AUTONOMY: 0ms EDGE AI INFERENCE
G HAZARD 04 // PHYSIOLOGY

Altered Gravity Fields

Weightlessness causes bone mineral density loss (-1.5%/mo), muscle atrophy, cardiovascular deconditioning, and cephalad fluid shifts producing SANS optic disc edema.

ARED LOAD: 380 LBS EQUIVALENT
E HAZARD 05 // ECLSS LIFE SUPPORT

Hostile Environments

Closed atmospheric loop inside spacecraft. Carbon dioxide accumulation (>3.0 mmHg) triggers hypercapnia and cognitive decline. Toxic trace contaminants scrubbed continuously.

CABIN pCO₂: 0.31 mmHg (SAFE)
